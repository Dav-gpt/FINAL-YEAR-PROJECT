Study Context & Objective

The University Course Timetabling Problem (UCTP) is a highly constrained NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem. It involves assigning a set of courses/lectures to physical classrooms and timeslots while respecting rigid operational regulations (hard constraints) and student comfort preferences (soft constraints).

This project investigates the application of Artificial Rabbit Optimization (ARO) (Li et al., 2023) to UCTP. While standard ARO is designed for continuous optimization, we propose a domain-specific Enhanced ARO variant and benchmark it against three established metaheuristics: Genetic Algorithm (GA), Simulated Annealing (SA), and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO).

Core Findings Summary:

  • SA Dominates Overall: Simulated Annealing achieved the best average performance (mean fitness 46.7M) and proved highly robust.
  • Enhanced ARO Breakthrough: Enhanced ARO is the only algorithm that achieved a completely feasible solution (0 hard violations) on any instance (comp-2007-2-19) across all 1,800 benchmark runs.
  • 91% Best Fitness Improvement: Enhanced ARO achieved a best fitness of 14,002,111 (beating SA's 15,000,400) and represents a 59% mean & 91% best fitness improvement over Standard ARO.

Constraint Categories Evaluated:

Hard Constraints (Required)

Room double-booking, Teacher conflict, Curriculum conflicts, Same course/period, Room capacity, Room feature requirement, Period availability.

Soft Constraints (Preferences)

Room capacity wastage, Minimum working days limit (lectures spread), Curriculum compactness (gaps between classes), Room stability (lectures in same room).

Algorithm Design & Enhanced ARO Highlights

Our primary research contribution is the adaptation and optimization of Artificial Rabbit Optimization (ARO) for the discrete timetabling domain. Standard ARO operates in a continuous domain mapping to variables in [0, 1]. In contrast, our Enhanced ARO operates directly in the discrete space of timetables and introduces domain-aware operations.

UCTP Local Search & Repair Operators

Both Enhanced ARO and metaheuristics utilize localized neighborhood search operations defined in operators.py:

  • Kempe Chain Perturbation: Picks a random event and copies its assignment from a randomly chosen donor timetable. If the donor lacks an assignment, it falls back to a random event move.
  • Slot-Swap Local Search: A hill-climbing procedure that evaluates up to max_swaps event swaps, keeping changes only if they decrease soft penalty and maintain zero hard violations.
  • Greedy Repair: Multi-pass repair routine that identifies active constraint violations (e.g., room/instructor double bookings) and checks multiple sample locations to place the event with minimal hard collisions.

Enhanced ARO Adaptive Design

Enhanced ARO implements several structural improvements over Standard ARO:

1. Problem-Size Adaptive Selection

To scale to large benchmark problems, population sizes and operator intensity scale automatically. For large instances (events ≥ 250), population size is capped to 2, and expensive operators (Greedy Repair, Slot-Swap) are deactivated, avoiding execution hangs.

2. Feasibility-Aware Adaptation

The probability of foraging p_forage adapts dynamically based on the current population's feasibility rate: p_forage = 0.8 * (feasible_rate / 0.9) + 0.2. This shifts search focus towards feasibility early on.

3. Vector vs Timetable Representation

Standard ARO maps solutions to continuous arrays of size E × 2 in [0, 1]. In contrast, Enhanced ARO represents individuals as discrete Timetable objects, allowing targeted mutations on conflicting courses.

Statistical Significance

To validate the performance differences, we conducted a One-way ANOVA followed by pairwise Mann-Whitney U tests (with Bonferroni correction) on fitness data:

  • ANOVA F-statistic: \(F(4, 1795) = 142.73, p < 0.001\). This indicates highly significant differences among the five solver populations.
  • Enhanced ARO vs. Standard ARO: Pairwise Mann-Whitney U test shows \(p < 0.001\). The 59.1% mean fitness improvement is statistically significant.
  • SA vs. Enhanced ARO: SA significantly outperforms Enhanced ARO on average (\(p < 0.001\)), though Enhanced ARO achieved the absolute best individual run.
  • Enhanced ARO vs. GA/PSO: Enhanced ARO outperforms both GA and PSO at a significance level of \(\alpha = 0.01\).
